# Cooling and Refrigerated Storage Standards

Refrigerated storage inhibits bacterial reproduction but does not sterilize food. Perishable cooked dishes must be chilled rapidly following cooking to minimize time spent traversing the danger zone.

Key parameters for refrigerated surplus food:
- The Two-Stage Cooling Rule requires cooling cooked food from 60°C down to 21°C within 2 hours, and subsequently from 21°C down to 5°C or lower within an additional 4 hours.
- Food kept under verified continuous cold storage at <= 5°C remains safe for rescue and redistribution for up to 24 to 48 hours depending on moisture and protein content.
- Uncovered cooling in refrigeration causes cross-contamination risk from drip condensation. All refrigerated surplus must be tightly covered with food-grade lids or cling wrap and dated with prep timestamp.
- Fluctuations in refrigerator temperature above 8°C accelerate microbial spoilage, requiring immediate downgrade to conservative ambient shelf-life limits.
