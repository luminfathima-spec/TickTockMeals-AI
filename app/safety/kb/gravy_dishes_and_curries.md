# Cooked Gravies, Sambar, Dal, and Meat Curries

Cooked gravies, lentil soups (sambar, rasam, dal tadka), and vegetable or meat curries are moist, high-protein media prone to rapid microbial growth if left unchecked in the danger zone.

Critical limits for curries and gravies:
- Ambient room storage: Curries and gravies held under ambient temperatures must be distributed and consumed within 3.5 to 4 hours from the finish of cooking.
- Continuous hot holding at >= 60°C maintains stability for up to 5 hours.
- Meat and poultry curries have stricter bacterial thresholds than vegetarian dals due to Salmonella and Clostridium perfringens risks. Meat curries held at ambient conditions beyond 3 hours must be flagged for elevated microbiological risk.
- Signs of sour fermentation, foam generation, bubbled surfaces, or rancid odors require immediate rejection for human consumption.
