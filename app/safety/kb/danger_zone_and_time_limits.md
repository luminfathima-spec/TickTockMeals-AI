# Temperature Danger Zone and Time Limits

The temperature range between 5°C and 60°C (41°F to 140°F) is designated as the Temperature Danger Zone. In this temperature zone, foodborne pathogenic microorganisms (such as Salmonella, Staphylococcus aureus, and Escherichia coli) multiply rapidly, doubling in number approximately every 20 minutes under favorable conditions.

Under ambient tropical storage conditions (typically 25°C to 35°C in Indian urban environments):
- Cooked perishable food left at ambient temperatures for up to 2 hours remains generally safe for immediate consumption or rapid reheating.
- Food held between 2 and 4 hours in the danger zone must be consumed immediately and must not be returned to refrigerated storage or re-held.
- Any cooked food held at ambient room temperature for more than 4 hours is considered hazardous due to accumulated bacterial toxins that may not be inactivated even by subsequent boiling or reheating. Such food must be declared unsafe for human consumption.
